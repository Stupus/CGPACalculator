What is a CGPA Calculator?

A CGPA Calculator is a simple utility tool designed to help students track their academic performance. Whether you need to figure out your Grade Point Average (GPA) for a single semester or your Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) across multiple semesters, this tool uses your specific grades and credit hours to provide an accurate result instantly.

How to calculate CGPA

Calculating your CGPA involves averaging the grade points you've earned across all completed semesters, weighted by the number of credits for each semester. To calculate manually:

  1. Multiply the GPA of each semester by the total credits of that semester.
  2. Add all those values together to get your total weighted grade points.
  3. Divide the total weighted grade points by the total number of credits attempted.

What is SGPA?

SGPA stands for Semester Grade Point Average. It represents your academic performance for one specific semester or term. It is calculated by dividing the total credit points earned in a semester by the total registered credits for that same semester.

SGPA vs CGPA

While SGPA measures your performance in a single term, CGPA is the broader measurement of your performance throughout your entire academic program. Your final degree classification is usually based on your CGPA rather than your individual SGPAs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is CGPA?

CGPA stands for Cumulative Grade Point Average. It is an overall assessment of a student's academic performance across all semesters or terms during their degree.

What is SGPA?

SGPA is Semester Grade Point Average, reflecting your performance solely based on the subjects taken during one specific academic term.

Why do credits matter?

Credits determine the weight of a subject. A 4-credit course heavily impacts your GPA more than a 2-credit course. Doing well in high-credit courses is crucial for a high CGPA.

Does every university use the same scale?

No. While a 10-point scale is very common in many countries (like India), some systems use a 4.0 scale (like in the US). Always verify your university's specific grading scale before converting grades.
